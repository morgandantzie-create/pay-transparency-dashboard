# Pay Transparency Dashboard

Gender pay gap reporting, EU Pay Transparency Directive analysis and remediation modelling, all in one tool. It runs as a static web page or as a Google Apps Script web app backed by Google Sheets.

> **All data is fictional.** Fernvale Systems and its ~480 employees are generated by a seeded script. **No AI is used**: every figure and insight is computed by deterministic, documented rules. This is not legal advice.

## Case study

### The problem
Pay equity reporting is usually an annual spreadsheet exercise. It produces a headline number but doesn't answer the questions that matter:
- **Why** does the gap exist? Is it because of who works where, or how people are paid?
- **Where** is the risk? The EU Pay Transparency Directive (EU) 2023/970 moves the focus to gaps within each *category of worker*, and a gap of 5% or more that can't be justified triggers a joint pay assessment.
- **What would it cost** to fix, and which option should we choose?
- **What can we tell an employee** who asks how their pay compares?

### What I built
One data model drives three views, each for a different audience:

| View | Audience | What they get |
|---|---|---|
| **Analyst** | Reward / People analytics, Talent | All six UK statutory measures; gap decomposition (structural vs unexplained); category heatmap with drill-down and joint-pay-assessment checklist; pay band governance; remediation scenario modeller; job advert salary ranges |
| **Leadership** | CFO / Exec | Headline KPIs, three-year trend with projection, prioritised risks, costed options side by side, group view across entities, print-to-PDF |
| **Employee** | Individual employee | Their band and position in it, compa-ratio, averages for people doing the same work (with small-group suppression), their rights, and a pay information request (preview of a planned Phase 2 add-on) |

### Design decisions worth talking about
- **Explain the gap, don't just report it.** A pooled regression on log hourly pay separates the *structural* gap (level, job family, location) from the *unexplained* like-for-like gap. The decomposition adds up exactly to the raw gap, so the waterfall chart is honest.
- **Raw vs explained at category level.** Each category is classed as *within threshold*, *over 5% but explained by objective factors* (document the justification), or *over 5% and unexplained* (action needed). That mirrors how the Directive treats justification.
- **Insights, not captions.** The insight engine (`src/core/insights.js`) explains causes and consequences rather than restating the chart. For example, it separates the part-time pro-rating effect from the bonus gap, and it spots that most below-band pay comes from one hiring cohort. Every insight is recalculated whenever a filter changes.
- **Scenarios recalculate everything.** Uplifts are applied to salaries and then *every* metric, including the regression, is recomputed from scratch. Nothing is estimated.
- **Privacy by design.**
  - Names are hidden by default in analyst views, which show IDs instead.
  - Employee-facing averages are suppressed when a group has fewer than 5 people.
  - The analyst view warns when a group is smaller than 5.
  - The Data & AI notice in the header sets out what approval would be needed before real names, or any AI processing of named data, could be used.
- **One codebase, two runtimes.** The core logic is plain JavaScript with no DOM or Google APIs. A small adapter switches between the static demo (data generated in the browser) and Google Sheets (via `google.script.run`). A parity test proves both produce identical numbers.

### Business value
- Statutory figures in seconds instead of days, with the methodology visible to anyone who asks.
- Risk is found by category before it becomes a joint pay assessment, an employment tribunal claim or a reputational problem.
- Decision-ready remediation options with annual cost and the effect on every metric.
- Consistent, defensible salary ranges for job adverts, as the Directive requires.

## Quick start

```bash
npm test          # 30 unit tests (hand-calculated fixtures)
node tests/parity.js
npm run dev       # http://localhost:5180/ui/index.html
npm run build     # dist/web/index.html (single file) + dist/apps-script/
```

There are no dependencies to install. The tests, dev server and build all use plain Node.js 18+.

## Methodology, in brief

| Measure | Definition used |
|---|---|
| Hourly pay | FTE salary ÷ (standard weekly hours × 52) |
| Mean / median pay gap | (men − women) ÷ men × 100, full-pay relevant employees only |
| Bonus gap | Same formula over bonus pay received in the 12 months to 5 April, among recipients; commission counts as bonus |
| Quartiles | Full-pay relevant employees ranked by hourly pay, split into four near-equal groups |
| Adjusted gap | (1 − e^β) × 100, where β is the Female coefficient in OLS of ln(hourly pay) on sex, level, family, location, tenure (capped at 10 years) and rating |
| Category of worker | Job family × level; threshold 5%; unexplained gap = difference in mean pay net of legitimate factors |

Simplifications compared with a real statutory submission are listed in [docs/TESTING.md](docs/TESTING.md#known-simplifications).

## Data, privacy and AI
- The data is synthetic, generated with a fixed seed. There is no connection to any employer system.
- No AI or machine-learning service is called, and no data leaves the browser or your own Google account.
- Pay information requests are shown as a **Phase 2 preview**. In a real deployment they would only go live after approval to use named employee data, including any AI-assisted drafting.
